  Why most GCC brands are running ROAS wrong — and how to fix it.

  Here's a mistake I see on almost every media plan I audit: one blended ROAS number for the whole account. Google, Meta, Snap, TikTok — all mixed together, reported as a single "win."

  It hides the truth.

  At SACO, when we split ROAS by channel instead of blending it, the picture flipped:
  • Google Search: 10.5×
  • Snapchat: 4.1×
  • TikTok: 3.3×
  • Meta (broad): 2.1×

  Blended, the account looked "fine." Separated, it was obvious that broad Meta campaigns were cannibalizing organic conversions — customers who were going to buy anyway, now counted as "acquired."

  We reallocated 40% of the Meta budget to Google Search. Result: revenue grew 62% in 60 days — with zero new customers. Same audience, same intent, just better attribution.

  The framework, in three steps:
  1. Tag every campaign by channel, audience segment, and funnel stage — no exceptions.
  2. Calculate incremental ROAS, not last-click ROAS. Ask: would this sale have happened without the ad?
  3. Reallocate ruthlessly toward the channels producing real incremental revenue, even if the "blended" number looks worse for a quarter.

  Blended ROAS is a vanity metric. Incremental, channel-level ROAS is the only number that should drive budget decisions.

  If your media plan reports one ROAS number for the whole account, you're not measuring performance — you're measuring noise.
